Is it even a bug? Whose bug is it anyway? What LLMs mistakes reveal about classifying software issue reports

UBC Okanagan Events

About the talk Your build breaks after a routine dependency update. Is that a bug? If so, whose bug is it, and where did it come from? Developers answer these questions every day when triaging issue reports, and getting them wrong can quietly contaminate the software bug datasets that much of software engineering research relies on. Prior studies have found that roughly one-third of reports labelled as bugs are not bugs at all. Large language models (LLMs) seem like a natural fit for automating this triage process. But when they fail, why do they fail, and what can those failures tell us? Anubhav Gupta presents an empirical study of six LLMs classifying 428 GitHub issues from the npm ecosyst
